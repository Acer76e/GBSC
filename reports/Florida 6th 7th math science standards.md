# Build Julia's Rational-Number Base Before Grade 7

Julia's most valuable work this year is to master the unit she is in right now: ratios, unit rates and percents (MA.6.AR.3.1 to 3.5). She should do it together with the fraction, decimal and percent fluency underneath it (MA.6.NSO.2.1 to 2.3, MA.6.NSO.3.5). Those skills hold up most of Grade 6 FAST Math, and Grade 7 builds directly on them through proportional relationships, percent applications and rational-number operations. Florida's Grade 6 math is more demanding than many online materials assume. It has **31 B.E.S.T. benchmarks**, and **all four integer operations are Grade 6 content in Florida**, while Common Core puts them in Grade 7. A generic "6th grade" quiz bank will therefore leave gaps. In science, Julia's current unit on cells, bacteria, protists and microbes maps to **SC.6.L.14.1 to 14.6 and SC.6.L.15.1** under the 2008 Next Generation Sunshine State Standards (NGSSS), which remain in force for 2026-27. **No state science test is given in Grade 6 or 7.** Everything she learns is tested cumulatively on the Grade 8 Statewide Science Assessment in spring 2029. Math is tested three times a year by FAST. Her next window is **PM2, November 30, 2026 to January 22, 2027**, and the one that counts for accountability is **PM3, May 3 to 28, 2027**, where Level 3 (on grade level) starts at a scale score of 229. The best evidence on what predicts later algebra success points to fractions and division. The best evidence on how to study points to spaced retrieval practice, which a multiple-choice app delivers well. Many official FDOE and CPALMS pages were blocked during research, so this report marks claims by how firmly they were verified. A register of the unverified items appears near the end.

## Grade 6 math is a year of rational numbers, ratios and one-step algebra

Florida Grade 6 math has **31 content benchmarks: 13 in Number Sense and Operations (NSO), 12 in Algebraic Reasoning (AR), 7 in Geometric Reasoning (GR) and 6 in Data Analysis and Probability (DP)** ([G6 Benchmark Clarification Document](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)). The NSO strand asks students to plot, order and compare rational numbers, including negatives, and to use absolute value as distance from zero. They must multiply and divide multi-digit decimals and fractions with procedural fluency, and find GCF, LCM, powers and prime factorizations. They convert among fractions, terminating decimals and percents (for example, 1 5/8 = 1.625 = 162.5%). Finally, they add, subtract, multiply and divide **integers** fluently by the end of the year. The AR strand covers translating words into expressions and inequalities, evaluating expressions with integers by order of operations, and writing equivalent expressions with the distributive property. It includes one-step equations with integer solutions and the full ratio cluster. That cluster includes part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios, unit rates (500 words in 3 minutes is 166 2/3 words per minute), ratio tables, percent problems solved as part/whole = percent/100, and conversions within one measurement system. The GR strand covers four-quadrant coordinates, area of triangles and composite figures (students must know A = 1/2 bh from memory), volume of prisms with fractional edges, and surface area from nets. The DP strand covers statistical questions, mean, median, mode and range, box plots, histograms, and how changing data affects center and spread. **Grade 6 has no probability** ([G6 BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)).

The clarification documents also set **FAST item limits**, which are useful difficulty settings for an app. Decimal computation items use no more than 5 total digits. Fraction products include at least one denominator over 20. GCF items stay within 1,000 and LCM factors within 25. Exponents go up to 5. The mean is computed only for data sets of 10 or fewer values. Multi-step fraction and decimal problems never mix the two forms in one item, and the mixed-form comparison items in MA.6.NSO.1.1 use only positive numbers ([G6 BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)). The seven Mathematical Thinking and Reasoning standards (MA.K12.MTR.1.1 to 7.1) are a lens embedded in every lesson rather than separately tested items ([FDOE Grade 7 Accelerated B1G-M](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/5199139/B1G-M_Grade_7_Accelerated.pdf)). The research found **no revision to the B.E.S.T. math benchmarks** since their February 2020 adoption. The 2026-27 changes are a new instructional-materials adoption and refreshed teacher guides, so the benchmark list above applies this year ([2026-27 B.E.S.T. EOC Fact Sheet](http://origin.fldoe.org/file/5662/2627BESTEOCFactSheet.pdf), *[snippet]*; [HMH adoption release](https://www.hmhco.com/about-us/press-releases/florida-department-of-education-approves-hmh-math-programs-for-grades-k-12)).

## Grade 7 adds proportionality, percent applications and probability

Florida Grade 7 math has **37 benchmarks: NSO 5, AR 12, GR 8 and DP 9** ([G7 Benchmark Clarification Document](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587896/G7_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)). The core of the year is extending Grade 6 integer work to **all rational numbers**, including negative fractions and decimals (MA.7.NSO.2.1 to 2.3). It also covers **proportional relationships**: testing for proportionality, finding the constant of proportionality k, graphing lines through the origin and translating between table, graph, equation (y = px) and description (MA.7.AR.4.1 to 4.5). **Multi-step percent problems** cover discount, markup, tax, tips, simple interest, percent change and percent error (MA.7.AR.3.1). Proportions and **unit conversion across systems**, customary to metric and currencies, are MA.7.AR.3.2 and 3.3. **Two-step equations** are MA.7.AR.2.2, and **one-step inequalities**, including flipping the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative, are MA.7.AR.2.1. New smaller topics include the laws of exponents with whole-number exponents (MA.7.NSO.1.1) and repeating decimals with at most two repeating digits (MA.7.NSO.1.2). Geometry adds area of trapezoids, parallelograms and polygons, circumference and area of circles, scale drawings (where areas scale by k squared), and cylinder surface area and volume. The data strand adds stem-and-leaf plots, comparing two populations, predictions from random samples of at least 100, and circle graphs. It also introduces Florida's **first probability unit**: sample space, likelihood, theoretical probability and simulations (MA.7.DP.2.1 to 2.4) ([G7 BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587896/G7_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)). Every one of these maps onto a Grade 6 prerequisite, which is why the order in the plan section matters. Proportional relationships rest on unit rate, ratio tables and the coordinate plane (6.AR.3.2, 6.AR.3.3, 6.GR.1.1). Percent applications rest on 6.AR.3.4 and 6.NSO.3.5. Two-step equations rest on 6.AR.2.2 to 2.4. Rational-number operations rest on 6.NSO.4.1 to 4.2 and 6.NSO.2.1 to 2.3 *[inferred mapping from the benchmark texts]*.

## Fractions, negative signs and the equals sign cause the most downstream trouble

The strongest prerequisite evidence comes from Siegler and colleagues (2012). **Fifth graders' knowledge of fractions and division predicted high-school algebra and overall math achievement**, even after controlling for parents' education and income. The finding held in a U.S. sample of 599 children and a U.K. sample of 3,677 ([APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/knowledge-of-fractions-and-long-division-predicts-long-term-math-success.html); [UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/early-grasp-fractions-long-division-predicts-high-school-math-achievement)). The federal REL Midwest fractions toolkit for **6th-grade** teachers treats "fractions" as all rational numbers, including decimals, percents and negative fractions ([REL Midwest](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/rel/fractions-grade-6)). That points to the same target as Florida's blueprint. On the algebra side, the research identifies three misconceptions. Students who read the **equals sign** as "the answer comes next," or the **negative sign** only as subtraction, struggle to solve equations ([Booth and Koedinger review](https://core.ac.uk/download/77937877.pdf)). Students also read a **variable** as a label ("s = students") rather than a number ([Carleton misconceptions summary](https://www.math.carleton.edu/comps2016-17/Adventures-in-Algebra/sites.google.com/site/adventuresinalgebra/for-teachers/misconceptions-addressed.html)). Seventh graders also confuse the *value* of an integer with its *size*, its absolute value ([METU abstract](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/07142a45-7267-4adf-870d-1310f825a5cc/prospective-teachers-knowledge-on-middle-school-students-possible-descriptions-of-integers)). Florida's own accelerated pacing reteaches "distributing a negative" at the start of Grade 7, which shows how often the gap appears ([OCSD pacing](https://core-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/3054757/7th_Grade_Accelerated_Math_2023-24-_Updated.pdf)).

Several classic errors make good wrong answers for an app, but they come from researcher background knowledge rather than a cited study *[unverified]*. Students may read a longer decimal as bigger (0.125 > 0.5). They may expect multiplication always to make numbers bigger. They may add numerators and denominators (1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5). They may read a 2:3 ratio as 2/3 of the whole instead of 2/5. They may confuse "percent of" with "percent increase," or say -3 < -5 because "3 is smaller." They may mix up area and perimeter units, or ignore outliers when choosing mean or median.

In science, the best-documented middle-school misconceptions involve **force and motion, conservation of energy and optics** ([Singh and Schunn, Pitt LRDC](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/singhschunn-jpteo09.pdf)), **heat versus temperature** ([OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/mod/oucontent/view.php?id=64798)), **seasons explained by distance from the Sun** ([Minnesota STEM Teacher Center](https://stemtc.scimathmn.org/node/1154)), and **plant mass coming from soil rather than carbon dioxide**, a belief that persists after photosynthesis is taught ([PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152202)). Misconceptions for Julia's current unit are useful for app distractors but are *[unverified]*: all microbes are harmful, viruses are cells, plant cells lack mitochondria, and only animals respire. The science *skills* students most need are identifying independent, dependent and controlled variables, reading distance-time graphs and data tables, computing speed, and answering in Claim-Evidence-Reasoning form *[inferred from the Florida pacing and course content above]*.

## A sequenced plan for Julia from now through summer 2027

The study method matters as much as the order. Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) rated only **practice testing and spaced practice** as "high utility." They rated highlighting, rereading and summarizing as low utility, even though those are what students do most ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)). The IES practice guide adds pairing worked examples with problems, quizzing to promote learning, and helping students judge what they don't know yet ([IES guide, WestEd copy](https://dww-library-files.wested.org/files/5198508.pdf)). For Julia this means short daily quizzes of 15 to 20 minutes. Each should be about 20% new material and 80% mixed review, with an explanation after every wrong answer. Review intervals should expand, roughly 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, then 2 to 3 weeks. A topic counts as mastered at about 80-90% across spaced attempts *[inferred design from the cited research]*.

**Phase 1, now to PM2 (November to mid-January).** Lock in the current units first. In math, the ratio cluster (6.AR.3.1 to 3.5) is only as strong as the fraction and decimal skills underneath, so each ratio session should be interleaved with fraction multiplication and division (6.NSO.2.2), decimal division (6.NSO.2.1) and fraction, decimal and percent conversion (6.NSO.3.5). If fractions break down, drop back to Khan Academy's Grade 5 fraction units before pushing on, as the Siegler evidence suggests. In science, quiz the current unit (6.L.14.1 to 14.4, 14.6, 15.1) and keep earlier units such as Nature of Science, speed and energy in the review rotation. A cross-subject module on speed as a unit rate (6.P.12.1 with 6.AR.3.2) serves both classes.

**Phase 2, January to April.** Turn to integers and rational-number sense (6.NSO.1.1 to 1.4, 4.1 to 4.2), with special care for comparing negatives and subtracting negatives. Then cover expressions and one-step equations (6.AR.1.1 to 1.4, 2.1 to 2.4), stressing the equals sign as balance and variables as numbers. These are the Grade 6 skills Grade 7 leans on hardest. Science follows the class pacing, likely Earth systems and forces, with the confirmed annually assessed benchmarks (6.E.7.4, 6.E.7.5, 6.L.14.2) weighted up.

**Phase 3, April to PM3 (May 3-28).** Run the geometry and data topics if the class has reached them (6.GR.1 to 2, 6.DP.1). Finish with cumulative mixed review in the FAST format using the official sample items.

**Summer 2027, about 8 weeks.** Spend two weeks restoring fraction and decimal fluency. Then one week each on integer operations, ratios and percents, and expressions and equations, all now practiced with negative fractions and decimals as Grade 7 requires. Follow with a preview of MA.7.AR.4 proportional relationships and MA.7.AR.3.1 percent applications, and a light introduction to probability, which is entirely new in Grade 7. For science, preview genetics (SC.7.L.16.1 to 16.2, building on cells) and ecology (SC.7.L.17), the Grade 7 topics closest to what Julia knows. Add one week on variables, graphs and the difference between replication and repetition *[proposed plan; no published Florida summer sequence was found]*.

## Conclusion

The research changes the usual picture of 6th-to-7th grade readiness in one important way. In Florida the hard pivot happens *inside* Grade 6, not at the Grade 7 boundary, because integers, ratios, percents and one-step equations are already Grade 6 benchmarks and together fill most of the FAST blueprint. Julia's current math unit is not one topic among many. It is the hinge for the Grade 7 Proportional Reasoning category and for percent applications. If her fraction and decimal base under it is shaky, no amount of Grade 7 preview will hold. The same logic applies to science. Cells and microbes look like a self-contained unit, but they are the foundation for Grade 7 genetics, and cell theory is one of the few benchmarks confirmed for the Grade 8 test.

For the app, the practical result is that she needs a **Florida-specific item bank tagged by benchmark code**, not a generic "grade 6" set. It should be weighted toward the P1 rows, set at the FDOE item limits, and run as spaced, mixed quizzes timed ahead of PM2 and PM3. Two dates are worth putting on the calendar: the December 2026 State Board science vote, which decides whether the Grade 7 science tags need updating, and the PM3 results in late May 2027, which will show whether acceleration is a realistic option or whether a summer of consolidation is the better investment.
