## What are all Grade 6 B.E.S.T. math benchmarks by strand (with clarifications/limits)?

### Takeaway
Grade 6 has **31 content benchmarks**: NSO 13, AR 12, GR 7, DP 6. Each comes with its own clarifications and FAST item limits. Grade 6 is heavy on rational-number sense, the **full set of integer operations** (add/subtract/multiply/divide are all Grade 6 in Florida, unlike Common Core, where they are Grade 7), fraction and decimal fluency, ratios/rates/percent, one-step equations, coordinate-plane geometry, area/volume/nets, and data displays (box plots, histograms). It also covers all seven MTR standards. Everything below comes from G6-BCD unless another source is cited.

### Cited Findings

**Strand: Number Sense & Operations (NSO)** — [G6-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.6.NSO.1.1**: Extend previous understanding of numbers to define rational numbers. Plot, order and compare rational numbers.
  - Positive and negative numbers are compared when they are in the *same* form. Mixed forms (fraction/decimal/percent) are used with *positive* numbers only. Students use <, >, =.
  - Item limits: same-form items must contain at least one negative value. Different-form items use only fractions that make terminating decimals.
  - Quiz idea: order −2.5, −2.05, 1.3; compare 3/8 and 0.4 and 37%.
- **MA.6.NSO.1.2**: Represent quantities with opposite direction using rational numbers. Compare them on a number line and explain the meaning of zero.
  - Uses vertical and horizontal number lines, with contexts of distance, temperature and finance ("lower, warmer, more in debt"). Items require no operations.
  - Example: elevations of +10 ft and −8 ft, with 0 = sea level.
- **MA.6.NSO.1.3**: Interpret absolute value as distance from zero. Find the absolute value of rational numbers.
  - Connects absolute value to mirror images and opposites. Items require no arithmetic.
- **MA.6.NSO.1.4**: Solve problems involving absolute value, including comparing absolute values.
  - Problems use **integers only** and **two or fewer operations**.
  - Example: a lemonade stand costs $10 to start and earns $5, so compare |−10| and |5|.
- **MA.6.NSO.2.1**: Multiply and divide positive multi-digit decimals to the thousandths using a standard algorithm, with procedural fluency.
  - Decimals have **no more than 5 total digits**. A number multiplied or divided by 0.1 or 0.01 has at least hundredths.
- **MA.6.NSO.2.2**: Multiply and divide positive fractions, including mixed numbers, with procedural fluency.
  - Fraction × fraction items include at least one **denominator > 20**. No unit fraction ÷ whole number, and no whole number ÷ unit fraction (those were Grade 5).
- **MA.6.NSO.2.3**: Solve multi-step real-world problems with the four operations on positive multi-digit decimals OR positive fractions, including mixed numbers.
  - Decimals and fractions are not mixed in one problem. Contexts exclude money, perimeter and area.
  - Items use at least 2 different operations, and not just addition and subtraction.
- **MA.6.NSO.3.1**: Find the GCF and LCM of two whole numbers.
  - **GCF within 1,000; LCM with factors up to 25.** Includes using GCF to simplify fractions.
  - Examples: dividing 15 flutes, 27 clarinets and 12 violins into equal groups is GCF. "Every 8 days vs every 12 days" is LCM.
- **MA.6.NSO.3.2**: Rewrite the sum of two composite whole numbers that share a common factor as the common factor times a sum (distributive property), e.g. 36 + 8 = 4(9 + 2). The common factor is ≤ 1,000.
- **MA.6.NSO.3.3**: Evaluate positive rational numbers and integers with natural-number exponents, **exponents up to 5**. Items have a positive rational base.
- **MA.6.NSO.3.4**: Express composite whole numbers as a product of prime factors with exponents (prime factorization, e.g. 72 = 2³·3²).
- **MA.6.NSO.3.5**: Rewrite positive rational numbers as fractions, terminating decimals and percents, to thousandths.
  - Example: 1 5/8 = 1.625 = 162.5%.
- **MA.6.NSO.4.1**: Add and subtract **integers** with procedural fluency.
  - Instruction moves from manipulatives and number lines to fluency by the end of Grade 6. Uses p − q = p + (−q).
  - Items include at least one negative, or subtraction where the subtrahend is larger than the minuend.
- **MA.6.NSO.4.2**: Multiply and divide **integers** with procedural fluency.
  - Covers the zero-divisor rule and the fact that every quotient of integers is rational: −(p/q) = (−p)/q = p/(−q).
  - Items include at least one negative integer.

**Strand: Algebraic Reasoning (AR)** — [G6-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.6.AR.1.1**: Translate words into algebraic expressions and expressions into words.
  - At most 2 unknowns, and no operations are required.
  - Example: 7.2x − 20 is daily profit at $7.20 per item with $20 in expenses.
- **MA.6.AR.1.2**: Translate a real-world description into an inequality of the form x > a, x < a, x ≥ a or x ≤ a, and graph it on a number line.
  - The variable can be on either side. Contexts are continuous.
  - Example: "at least 83" becomes g ≥ 83.
- **MA.6.AR.1.3**: Evaluate expressions using substitution and order of operations, **with integers**. Up to 3 variables.
  - Example: evaluate 2a³ − b/5 with a = −1 and b = 15.
- **MA.6.AR.1.4**: Use properties (associative, commutative, distributive) to write equivalent expressions with integer coefficients, in 1–2 variables.
  - Examples: 5(3x + 1) = 15x + 5; 2x + 3x = 5x.
- **MA.6.AR.2.1**: Given an equation or inequality and a set of integer values, decide which values make it true.
  - The variable can appear in more than one term or on both sides. Sets can be shown in braces.
  - Example: which of {−4, −2, 0, 1} make x + 1 < 2 true?
- **MA.6.AR.2.2**: Write and solve one-step equations using addition and subtraction (x + p = q), with all terms and solutions integers.
  - Items include a negative integer in the equation or the solution. The variable can be on either side.
  - Example: −35 + x = 17.
- **MA.6.AR.2.3**: Write and solve one-step equations using multiplication and division (px = q, x/p = q, p ≠ 0), with integer terms and solutions.
- **MA.6.AR.2.4**: Find the unknown decimal or fraction in an equation that relates three numbers with any of the four operations, with the unknown in any position.
  - Positive rationals only, and items do not mix forms. Instruction emphasizes mental math and relational thinking.
- **MA.6.AR.3.1**: Write and interpret ratios (a/b, a to b, a:b, b ≠ 0), both part-to-part and part-to-whole.
  - a and b are whole numbers. The order in the context does not fix the order of the ratio.
- **MA.6.AR.3.2**: Find a rate and the unit rate.
  - Example: 500 words in 3 minutes is 166⅔ words per minute.
  - No customary/metric conversions, and items require no unit conversion.
- **MA.6.AR.3.3**: Generate or complete two- or three-column tables of equivalent ratios (part:part:whole), e.g. paint-mixture or conversion charts.
- **MA.6.AR.3.4**: Use ratio reasoning to solve percent problems (part/whole = percent/100). Items ask for the percent, the part or the whole.
  - Example: 32 g of protein in a 340 g shake.
- **MA.6.AR.3.5**: Solve ratio, rate and unit-rate problems, including comparisons, mixtures, ratios of lengths, and conversions **within one measurement system**.
  - Tools: tables, tape diagrams, number lines. No conversions between systems.

**Strand: Geometric Reasoning (GR)** — [G6-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.6.GR.1.1**: Plot rational-number ordered pairs in all four quadrants and on the axes. Identify the x- or y-axis as the line of reflection when two points have opposite coordinates.
  - First-quadrant items use non-whole-number coordinates.
- **MA.6.GR.1.2**: Find the distance between points that share an x- or y-coordinate. Integer coordinates, any quadrant.
- **MA.6.GR.1.3**: Solve problems by plotting points, including the perimeter or area of a rectangle with sides parallel to the axes, and finding a missing fourth vertex.
- **MA.6.GR.2.1**: Derive the formula for the area of a right triangle from a rectangle, then apply A = ½bh to any triangle. Students must **know the formula from memory**. Items give the vertical height.
- **MA.6.GR.2.2**: Find the area of quadrilaterals and composite figures by breaking them into triangles and rectangles, including missing dimensions.
  - Dimensions are positive rationals. Given quadrilaterals are not rectangles or squares. Rectangle and triangle formulas are memorized.
- **MA.6.GR.2.3**: Find the volume of right rectangular prisms with positive rational edge lengths, or a missing edge. At least one edge is a fraction or decimal.
- **MA.6.GR.2.4**: Find the surface area of right rectangular prisms and right rectangular pyramids **using nets**.
  - Items do not require the surface-area formula. Pyramid triangle heights are given. Items may ask students to match a solid to its net.

**Strand: Data Analysis & Probability (DP)**. Grade 6 has no probability. — [G6-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.6.DP.1.1**: Recognize and write a statistical question that produces numerical data, e.g. "How many minutes did you spend on math homework last night?"
- **MA.6.DP.1.2**: Find and interpret the mean, median, mode and range. Data are positive rationals.
  - **No more than 20 data points**, and the mean is computed only for **10 or fewer points**.
  - Example: the set {15, 0, 32, 24, 0, 17, 42, 0, 29, 120, 0, 20} has mode 0.
- **MA.6.DP.1.3**: Read a box plot (minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum) and describe spread using range, IQR, halves and quarters. Vocabulary: symmetry, skewed, outlier.
- **MA.6.DP.1.4**: Describe histograms and line plots qualitatively: symmetry, skewness, gaps, clusters, outliers, range. No statistic other than range is calculated.
- **MA.6.DP.1.5**: Create box plots and histograms with titles, labels, scales and units. 20 or fewer data points. Discuss truthful vs. deceptive data.
- **MA.6.DP.1.6**: Describe how changing data values affects the mean and median, and the range and IQR. Choose the appropriate measure.

## What are all Grade 7 B.E.S.T. math benchmarks by strand (with clarifications/limits)?

### Takeaway
Grade 7 has **37 content benchmarks**: NSO 5, AR 12, GR 8, DP 9 (DP.1 statistics ×5, DP.2 probability ×4). The year is organized around proportional relationships, operations with rational numbers in every form (including negative fractions and decimals), percent applications, two-step equations, one-step inequalities, circles, scale drawings, cylinders, comparing populations, sampling, circle graphs, and the first probability unit. Everything below comes from G7-BCD (2023) unless another source is cited.

### Cited Findings

**Strand: Number Sense & Operations (NSO)** — [G7-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587896/G7_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.7.NSO.1.1**: Know and apply the Laws of Exponents to evaluate and rewrite numerical expressions, with **whole-number exponents and rational bases**.
  - Students build the laws from examples. In problems of the form aᵖ/aᵠ, p − q must be a whole number, so there are no negative exponents (those are Grade 8).
  - Items must use at least one law of exponents.
- **MA.7.NSO.1.2**: Rewrite rational numbers as fractions, mixed numbers, **repeating decimals** and percents to solve problems.
  - Items focus on fractions that become repeating decimals, with **no more than two repeating digits**.
  - Example: is 17/3 equal to the calculator's 5.6666666667?
- **MA.7.NSO.2.1**: Multi-step order of operations with rational numbers, including nested grouping symbols, whole-number exponents and absolute value.
  - **6 or fewer steps**, decimals to hundredths, exponents ≤ 3.
- **MA.7.NSO.2.2**: Add, subtract, multiply and divide **rational numbers** with procedural fluency.
  - Items include at least one negative value and/or numbers in mixed forms, and may have a missing term.
- **MA.7.NSO.2.3**: Solve real-world problems using any of the four operations with rational numbers. Items can have more than one step.

**Strand: Algebraic Reasoning (AR)** — [G7-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587896/G7_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.7.AR.1.1**: Add and subtract linear expressions (ax ± b) with rational coefficients. Items combine only two expressions.
  - Example: (7x − 4) − (2 − ½x) = 7½x − 6. The fraction in the source was garbled; the structure is clear.
- **MA.7.AR.1.2**: Decide whether two linear expressions with rational coefficients are equivalent.
  - Example: is (2/3)(6 − x) − 3x equivalent to 8 − (5/3)x? The source fraction was partly garbled.
- **MA.7.AR.2.1**: Write and solve **one-step inequalities** of the forms px > q, x/p > q, x ± p > q and p ± x > q, with rational p and q and any inequality symbol. Represent solutions algebraically or graphically.
  - Taught through the properties of inequality, including **flipping the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative**. The variable can be on either side.
- **MA.7.AR.2.2**: Write and solve **two-step equations** px ± q = r and p(x ± q) = r with rational terms. The variable can be on either side.
- **MA.7.AR.3.1**: Solve **multi-step percent problems**: discounts, markups, **simple interest, tax, tips, fees, percent increase and decrease, percent error**.
  - Examples: if 23% of juniors take art, find the ratio of art to non-art students. A 3:2 boy:girl ratio means what percent are boys?
- **MA.7.AR.3.2**: Solve real-world problems with proportions.
  - Examples: if 4 lawns take 3 h 45 min, how long do 35 take? 10 km ≈ 6.2 mi, so how many km is a 13.1 mi half-marathon?
- **MA.7.AR.3.3**: Convert units **across** measurement systems (customary ↔ metric) and currencies, for length, area, weight, mass, volume and money. Exchange rates are given.
- **MA.7.AR.4.1**: Decide whether two quantities are **proportional** from a table, graph or description, using the constant ratio and a line through the origin. Items give no equation.
- **MA.7.AR.4.2**: Find the **constant of proportionality** from a table, graph or description.
  - Examples: a line through (0,0) and (5,2) gives k = 2/5. Babysitting at $9 per hour gives e = 9h.
- **MA.7.AR.4.3**: Graph proportional relationships from a table, equation (y = px) or description.
- **MA.7.AR.4.4**: Translate any representation of a proportional relationship (description, graph, table, equation y = px) into another.
  - Examples: m = 60h; h = (1/9)e.
- **MA.7.AR.4.5**: Solve real-world problems with proportional relationships.
  - Example: 1,407 miles at 23.1 mpg with a 17.5-gal tank. How many fill-ups?

**Strand: Geometric Reasoning (GR)** — [G7-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587896/G7_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.7.GR.1.1**: Apply formulas for the area of **trapezoids, parallelograms and rhombi**, linked back to rectangles and triangles.
  - These formulas are **not memorized**; they appear on the reference sheet. Only bases and vertical height are labeled.
- **MA.7.GR.1.2**: Find the area of polygons and composite figures by breaking them into triangles and quadrilaterals.
  - No coordinate-plane figures and no missing dimensions. Composite figures have more than 4 sides.
- **MA.7.GR.1.3**: Explore C/d = π (circumference ÷ diameter) and apply the **circumference** formula. Answers can be in terms of π or approximate.
- **MA.7.GR.1.4**: Explore and apply the **area of a circle**, including fractional parts of a circle.
  - Example: 2 of 6 slices of a 12-inch pizza.
- **MA.7.GR.1.5**: Solve problems with **scale drawings and scale factors**: find the scale factor, or find dimensions given a scale factor.
  - If the length scale factor is k, then **areas scale by k²**.
- **MA.7.GR.2.1**: Find the surface area of a **right circular cylinder from its net**. The formula is not memorized and items do not require it.
- **MA.7.GR.2.2**: Solve real-world surface-area problems for cylinders.
  - The formula is given, and some contexts use 1 or 2 bases. Students never solve for the radius.
- **MA.7.GR.2.3**: Solve **volume of right circular cylinder** problems. The formula is not memorized and students never solve for the radius.

**Strand: Data Analysis & Probability (DP)** — [G7-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587896/G7_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf)
- **MA.7.DP.1.1**: Choose the best measure of center (mean or median) or variation (range or IQR) for a context, accounting for outliers.
  - Displays: histograms, line plots, box plots, **stem-and-leaf plots**. Stem-and-leaf plots are new in Grade 7.
- **MA.7.DP.1.2**: Compare **two populations** using center and variability from two data displays.
  - 20 or fewer data points, and the mean is computed only for 10 or fewer.
- **MA.7.DP.1.3**: Use proportions with a **random sample** of categorical data to predict for a population. The sample size is **at least 100**.
  - Example: 6 defective out of 600, so predict defects in 4,300.
- **MA.7.DP.1.4**: Build and interpret **circle graphs** using proportional reasoning. **6 or fewer categories**.
- **MA.7.DP.1.5**: Choose and create an appropriate display: histogram, bar chart, circle graph, line plot, box plot or stem-and-leaf plot.
- **MA.7.DP.2.1**: Find the **sample space** of a simple experiment: coin, die, card, marbles, spinner.
  - Dice and decks can be non-standard, for example dice with other side counts or decks of colors or letters.
- **MA.7.DP.2.2**: Interpret the likelihood of an event from its probability (0 to 1; near 0 is unlikely, near 1 is likely) and compare probabilities. Uses P(event) notation.
- **MA.7.DP.2.3**: Find the **theoretical probability** of a simple event, as a fraction, decimal or percent.
- **MA.7.DP.2.4**: Run a **simulation** to find experimental probability and compare it to theoretical probability.
  - With more trials, experimental results come closer to theoretical. Items give at least 100 trials.
  - Example: toss a coin 1,000 times and compare to 0.5.

### Inferences
- Grade 7 quiz clusters: (1) exponent laws; (2) repeating decimals and conversions; (3) order of operations with absolute value; (4) rational-number operations; (5) linear expressions; (6) one-step inequalities and two-step equations; (7) percent applications such as tax, tip, markup, discount, simple interest, percent change and percent error; (8) proportions and unit conversion across systems; (9) proportional relationships: test, k, graph, translate; (10) area of quadrilaterals and composite figures; (11) circles; (12) scale drawings; (13) cylinders; (14) statistics: choosing measures, comparing populations, sampling; (15) circle graphs and choosing a display; (16) probability.

## What are the Florida middle school math course codes/paths and which benchmarks does each accelerated course compress? How are students placed?

### Takeaway
The standard path is **M/J Grade 6 Mathematics → M/J Grade 7 Mathematics → M/J Grade 8 Pre-Algebra → Algebra 1 in Grade 9**. The common accelerated path is **M/J Accelerated Mathematics Grade 6 (1205020) → M/J Accelerated Mathematics Grade 7 (1205050) → Algebra 1 or Algebra 1 Honors in Grade 8**. This path compresses Grades 6, 7 and 8 into two years. Grade 6 Accelerated teaches all of Grade 6 plus about half of Grade 7: rational-number operations, expressions, inequalities, percent, proportions, basic statistics and probability. Grade 7 Accelerated teaches the remaining Grade 7 benchmarks plus **all of Grade 8**. Placement criteria are set by **districts**. I found no statewide automatic-enrollment rule for middle school.

## What topics are new in Grade 7 relative to Grade 6, and which Grade 6 skills are prerequisites?

### Takeaway
In Florida, integer operations are already Grade 6 (NSO.4.1–4.2). Grade 7 extends operations to **all rational numbers**, including negative fractions and decimals. Its truly new topics are proportional relationships, multi-step percent applications, two-step equations, one-step inequalities with rational numbers, laws of exponents, repeating decimals, circles, scale drawings, cylinders, stem-and-leaf plots, sampling, circle graphs and probability. Each one builds directly on a specific Grade 6 benchmark (mapping below; benchmark text from G6-BCD and G7-BCD).

### Cited Findings
Each row lists a Grade 7 topic and the Grade 6 prerequisites from [G6-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587900/G6_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf) and [G7-BCD](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/4587896/G7_-_Benchmark_Clarification_Document.pdf):
- **Rational-number operations** (7.NSO.2.2–2.3) build on integer operations (6.NSO.4.1–4.2), positive fraction and decimal fluency (6.NSO.2.1–2.3) and absolute value (6.NSO.1.3–1.4).
- **Order of operations with absolute value and nested grouping** (7.NSO.2.1) builds on 6.AR.1.3, 6.NSO.3.3 and 6.NSO.1.3.
- **Laws of exponents** (7.NSO.1.1) builds on evaluating powers with exponents up to 5 (6.NSO.3.3) and prime factorization with exponents (6.NSO.3.4).
- **Repeating decimals and mixed numbers** (7.NSO.1.2) builds on terminating fraction/decimal/percent conversion (6.NSO.3.5).
- **Combining linear expressions with rational coefficients** (7.AR.1.1–1.2) builds on properties with integer coefficients (6.AR.1.4) and GCF and distributing (6.NSO.3.2).
- **Two-step equations** (7.AR.2.2) builds on one-step integer equations (6.AR.2.2–2.3) and unknowns with fractions and decimals (6.AR.2.4).
- **One-step inequalities solved algebraically** (7.AR.2.1) builds on writing and graphing x > a (6.AR.1.2) and testing values (6.AR.2.1).
- **Percent applications** such as markup, discount, tax, tip, simple interest, percent change and percent error (7.AR.3.1) build on basic percent from ratio (6.AR.3.4) and percent forms (6.NSO.3.5).
- **Proportions and unit conversion across systems** (7.AR.3.2–3.3) build on ratios, rates, unit rate and conversions within one system (6.AR.3.1–3.5).
- **Proportional relationships** (k, graphs through the origin, y = px; 7.AR.4.x) build on unit rate (6.AR.3.2), ratio tables (6.AR.3.3) and the coordinate plane (6.GR.1.1).
- **Area of trapezoids, parallelograms, rhombi and polygons** (7.GR.1.1–1.2) builds on triangle area and decomposition (6.GR.2.1–2.2).
- **Circles** (7.GR.1.3–1.4) build on ratio and unit-rate thinking (C/d = π) and rectangle area.
- **Scale drawings with k² for area** (7.GR.1.5) build on ratios of lengths (6.AR.3.5) and area (6.GR.2.2).
- **Cylinder surface area and volume** (7.GR.2.x) build on nets and surface area of prisms and pyramids (6.GR.2.4) and prism volume (6.GR.2.3).
- **Choosing a measure, comparing populations, stem-and-leaf plots** (7.DP.1.1–1.2) build on 6.DP.1.2–1.6.
- **Sampling predictions and circle graphs** (7.DP.1.3–1.4) build on percent and ratio (6.AR.3.4).
- **Probability** (7.DP.2.1–2.4) is entirely new. Grade 6 DP has no probability benchmark. It uses fraction/decimal/percent forms (6.NSO.3.5).
- The G7 accelerated pacing explicitly notes reteaching "Expressions and Combining Like Terms... focusing on distributing a negative" as a prerequisite at the start of Grade 7 Accelerated. — [OCSD pacing](https://core-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/2897/OCSD/3054757/7th_Grade_Accelerated_Math_2023-24-_Updated.pdf)

### Inferences
- Florida vs Common Core: Florida puts integer add/subtract/multiply/divide in Grade 6, while CCSS puts them in Grade 7 (7.NS). Florida Grade 7 also includes the laws of exponents (whole-number exponents), repeating decimals and cross-system unit conversion. Grade 6 includes rectangular-pyramid nets but no probability. Quiz banks based on Common Core will be misaligned on these points.
- Highest-value Grade 6 review topics before Grade 7: integer operations, fraction and decimal fluency, ratio tables and unit rate, percent of a quantity, one-step equations, and the distributive property.
