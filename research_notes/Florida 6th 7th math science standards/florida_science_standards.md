## 1. Which science standards are in force for 2026-27?

### Takeaway
The **Next Generation Sunshine State Standards (NGSSS) for Science, adopted February 2008, are still the standards in force and taught during 2026-27.** In September 2026 FLDOE released a **draft rewrite** of the K-12 science standards. The State Board of Education is expected to vote on it in **December 2026**. If adopted, it would be **phased in over four years**, with teacher training starting in **spring semester 2026-27**. So a current 6th grader is taught and tested on NGSSS this year. Her 7th-grade year (2027-28) *may* be the first rollout year in some form, but the grade-by-grade schedule has not been published.

### Cited Findings
- The NGSSS for Science were adopted by the Florida State Board of Education in February 2008, and they are still the current science standards. Florida is rewriting its K-12 science standards for the first time since 2008. — [Wikipedia: Sunshine State Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_State_Standards); [FLDOE Science page](https://www.fldoe.org/academics/standards/subject-areas/math-science/science/)
- Draft standards were released for public comment on **September 25, 2026**, with comments open through October 2026. The **State Board vote is expected in December 2026**. — [Florida Trend, Oct 15 2026](https://www.floridatrend.com/articles/2026/10/15/new-k-12-science-curriculum-scrutinized-december-vote-by-board-ed-expected/); [Central Florida Public Media, Oct 1 2026](https://www.cfpublic.org/education/2026-10-01/floridas-proposed-science-standards-sideline-evolution-climate-change)

- Unlike ELA and math, which moved to B.E.S.T., science was **not** replaced during 2019-2025. Science course descriptions on CPALMS keep the NGSSS science benchmarks and add B.E.S.T. ELA/Math "expectations" (ELA.K12.EE, MA.K12.MTR) as related benchmarks. **[recall – verify on CPALMS]**

### Inferences
- **For the study app this year (6th grade, 2026-27): build everything on NGSSS.** Even if the Board adopts the new standards in December 2026, classroom instruction in 2026-27 will not change, because training only starts in spring 2027.
- For 7th grade (2027-28), NGSSS will most likely still apply. A four-year phase-in usually begins with some grades or courses, and testing changes lag behind. The Grade 8 Statewide Science Assessment she takes in spring 2029 would probably still be NGSSS-based, or a transition version. Recheck after the December 2026 vote.
- Core topics (cells, body systems, ecology, weather, plate tectonics, forces, energy, waves) appear in essentially every science framework. Quiz content on them stays useful even if the codes change. The topics most at risk of being reworded are **evolution (SC.7.L.15)** and **climate-related content**.

## 2. Florida middle school science courses and codes

### Takeaway
Florida offers two families of middle school science courses:
- **The integrated "Comprehensive Science" series**, with one course per grade: Comprehensive Science 1 (gr 6), 2 (gr 7) and 3 (gr 8), each with an Advanced version.
- **Discipline-based courses**: M/J Earth/Space Science, M/J Life Science and M/J Physical Science, each with an Advanced version.

Comprehensive Science 1 holds roughly the grade-6 NGSSS benchmarks and Comprehensive Science 2 the grade-7 ones. Each discipline course pulls one discipline's benchmarks from all of grades 6-8. For example, M/J Life Science contains SC.6.L.14/15, SC.7.L.15/16/17 and SC.8.L.18.

## 3. District patterns, and what this student's class matches

### Takeaway
Most large districts and FLVS use the **Comprehensive Science 1 → 2 → 3** sequence. Some districts, Broward historically among them, have used a **discipline-per-grade** sequence: grade 6 Earth/Space, grade 7 Life, grade 8 Physical. A 6th-grade class studying **bacteria, protists, microbes and cells** matches **SC.6.L.14 (cells, cell theory, organelles, body systems, infectious agents) and SC.6.L.15.1 (classification: domains and kingdoms)**. These are grade-6 benchmarks that sit in Comprehensive Science 1, and also in M/J Life Science if her district teaches Life in grade 6.

### Cited Findings
- **Miami-Dade:** the district K-12 Science Plan has a grade-8 year-at-a-glance for M/J Comprehensive Science 3, which points to a Comprehensive sequence. — [M-DCPS K-12 Science Plan 2019](https://api.dadeschools.net/WMSFiles/42/Elementary/K-12%20Science%20Plan%20-2019.pdf)
- **Broward:** a 2015-16 district grade 6 map is titled "6TH Grade Science … M/J Earth Science", and a separate "7TH Grade Life Science: Regular & Advanced Curriculum Map" exists. That fits a grade 6 Earth/Space → grade 7 Life → grade 8 Physical pattern. Broward's current 6-12 scope and sequence page lists discipline courses (Earth, Life, Physical, plus a grade-6 "GEARS" course). — [Broward Gr 6 Earth Science map (2015-16)](http://www.broward.k12.fl.us/ospa/ospa-central2/_sip_plan_evidence/2017/1891_12182015_2015_16_mj_earth_science_curriculum_map.pdf); [Broward Gr 7 Life Science map](http://www.broward.k12.fl.us/ospa/ospa-central2/_sip_plan_evidence/2017/1891_12182015_2015_16_mj_life_science_curriculum_map.pdf); [Broward Science Scope and Sequence 6-12](https://www.browardschools.com/bcps-departments/innovative-learning/bcps-ela-curriculum-scope-and-sequence/science-scope-and-sequence-for-6-12)

- **Palm Beach and St. Lucie** publish Comprehensive Science 1/2 standards documents and scope and sequences, which means they run Comprehensive sequences. — [Palm Beach 2002040](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1726077101/palmbeachschoolsorg/k8ijfbyg4to7xi5rx0ts/gradescience.pdf); [St. Lucie SC06](https://www.stlucie.k12.fl.us/curriculum/P_SC/SC06.pdf)
- **FLVS:** Comprehensive 1/2/3. — [FLVS progression](https://www.flvs.net/docs/default-source/full-time/flvs_full_time_ms_course_progression.pdf)
- Benchmark match for microbes: SC.6.L.14.6 covers comparing types of infectious agents that may infect the human body, including viruses (and bacteria, fungi and parasites). — [FLDOE Interim Assessment Item Specs, Science 6-8](https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/5423/urlt/FL-Item-Spec-SCI-6-8-WT-r2g.pdf); [flrules course descriptions](https://flrules.org/gateway/readRefFile.asp?refId=222&filename=Grades+PreK-12+Science.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Which course she is most likely in:**
  - If her district is a Comprehensive district (Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, St. Johns, St. Lucie, FLVS and most others), she is in Comprehensive Science 1 (2002040), or 2002050 if Advanced, and is in the **life-science unit: SC.6.L.14.1–14.6 and SC.6.L.15.1**.
    - Protists and bacteria fit **SC.6.L.15.1** (domains Archaea, Bacteria and Eukarya; kingdoms; Linnaean hierarchy).
    - Microbes as pathogens fit **SC.6.L.14.6**.
    - Cells fit **SC.6.L.14.1–14.4**.
  - Some Comprehensive 1 pacing guides teach Life first and others teach it last.
  - Her **6th-grade year will also include** Nature of Science, Earth (weathering, erosion, landforms, weather, climate, atmosphere, water cycle, heat transfer) and Physical (kinetic/potential energy, speed graphs, forces, gravity).
- **What she will likely see in 7th grade:**
  - Comprehensive districts: **Comprehensive Science 2**. That means plate tectonics, Earth's layers, rock cycle, geologic time and human impact (SC.7.E.6); light, waves and heat/energy transformations (SC.7.P.10–11); and evolution and fossils, heredity and genetics (DNA, Punnett squares, mitosis vs meiosis, biotechnology), and ecology (food webs, symbiosis, limiting factors) (SC.7.L.15–17).
  - Discipline-based districts (historically Broward): **M/J Life Science in grade 7**. That covers the whole SC.6.L/SC.7.L/SC.8.L life block, including deeper cells and body systems plus genetics, evolution, ecology, and photosynthesis/respiration.
- If she is in a discipline-based district and doing Life Science in grade 6, her grade 7 course would probably be Earth/Space or Physical. The parent should check the course name on the schedule or report card. Course number 2002040 means Comprehensive Science 1, and 2000010 means M/J Life Science.

### GRADE 6 (M/J Comprehensive Science 1, 2002040)

#### Nature of Science (Body of Knowledge N)

**Big Idea 1: The Practice of Science**
- **SC.6.N.1.1**: Define a problem; use reference materials; plan and carry out investigations (systematic observations or experiments); identify variables; collect, organize and interpret data in charts, tables and graphs; make predictions; defend conclusions. *Plain:* run a full investigation, from question to conclusion. **[recall – verify]**
- **SC.6.N.1.2**: Explain why scientific investigations should be replicable. *Plain:* others must be able to repeat the work and get similar results.
- **SC.6.N.1.3**: Explain the difference between an experiment and other types of scientific investigation, and the benefits and limitations of each. *Plain:* experiment (controls variables) vs. observation, field study or model.
- **SC.6.N.1.4**: Discuss, compare and negotiate methods, results and explanations among groups doing the same investigation.
- **SC.6.N.1.5**: Recognize that science involves creativity in designing experiments and in creating explanations that fit evidence.

**Big Idea 2: The Characteristics of Scientific Knowledge**
- **SC.6.N.2.1**: Distinguish science from other activities involving thought. *Plain:* science vs. opinion, belief or pseudoscience; science must be testable.
- **SC.6.N.2.2**: Explain that scientific knowledge is durable *because* it is open to change as new evidence appears.
- **SC.6.N.2.3**: Recognize that scientists come from all kinds of backgrounds and have varied talents, interests and goals.

**Big Idea 3: The Role of Theories, Laws, Hypotheses, and Models**
- **SC.6.N.3.1**: A scientific theory is a well-supported, widely accepted explanation of nature, not just one person's claim. "Theory" in science differs from everyday use.
- **SC.6.N.3.2**: A scientific law describes a specific relationship under given conditions in nature. It is different from a societal law.
- **SC.6.N.3.3**: Give several examples of scientific laws (e.g., Law of Gravity, Law of Conservation of Energy/Mass, Newton's laws).
- **SC.6.N.3.4**: Identify the role of models in the context of the grade 6 benchmarks (e.g., cell models, globe, weather maps).

*Vocabulary:* hypothesis, independent (test) variable, dependent (outcome) variable, controlled variables/constants, control group, trial, data, qualitative, quantitative, observation, inference, prediction, conclusion, replication, experiment, investigation, theory, law, model, evidence, bias.

#### Earth and Space Science (Body of Knowledge E)

**Big Idea 6: Earth Structures**
- **SC.6.E.6.1** *(cited above)*: Earth's surface is built up and torn down by physical and chemical weathering, erosion and deposition.
- **SC.6.E.6.2**: Recognize the variety of landforms on Earth's surface (coastlines, dunes, rivers, mountains, glaciers, deltas, lakes) and relate them to Florida. **[recall – verify]**

*Vocabulary:* weathering (mechanical/physical, chemical), erosion, deposition, sediment, agents of erosion (water, wind, ice/glaciers, gravity), abrasion, frost wedging, oxidation, acid rain, karst, sinkhole, aquifer, barrier island, delta, dune, landform.

**Big Idea 7: Earth Systems and Patterns** *(all [recall – verify] except 7.4)*
- **SC.6.E.7.1**: Differentiate radiation, conduction and convection, the three ways heat moves through Earth's systems.
- **SC.6.E.7.2**: Investigate and apply how the cycling of water between atmosphere and hydrosphere affects weather patterns and climate (water cycle).
- **SC.6.E.7.3**: Describe how global patterns such as the jet stream and ocean currents influence local weather (temperature, air pressure, wind direction and speed, precipitation).
- **SC.6.E.7.4** *(cited)*: Differentiate and show interactions among the geosphere, hydrosphere, cryosphere, atmosphere and biosphere.
- **SC.6.E.7.5**: Explain how the sun's energy drives global atmospheric movement and the temperature differences between air, water and land (uneven heating, sea and land breezes).
- **SC.6.E.7.6**: Differentiate between weather (short-term) and climate (long-term).
- **SC.6.E.7.7**: Investigate how natural disasters have affected human life in Florida (hurricanes, tornadoes, floods, wildfires, sinkholes).
- **SC.6.E.7.8**: Describe ways humans protect themselves from hazardous weather and sun exposure.
- **SC.6.E.7.9**: Describe how the composition and structure of the atmosphere protects life and insulates the planet (layers, ozone layer, greenhouse effect).

*Vocabulary:* radiation, conduction, convection, convection current, evaporation, condensation, precipitation, transpiration, runoff, infiltration, humidity, dew point, air pressure, barometer, front (cold/warm/stationary/occluded), air mass, high/low pressure, jet stream, Gulf Stream, ocean current, global winds, Coriolis effect, sea breeze/land breeze, troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, ozone, greenhouse effect, climate zone, hurricane, storm surge, UV radiation, SPF.

#### Physical Science (Body of Knowledge P) *(all [recall – verify] except 13.1)*

**Big Idea 11: Energy Transfer and Transformations**
- **SC.6.P.11.1**: Explore the Law of Conservation of Energy by distinguishing potential from kinetic energy. Identify where kinetic energy becomes potential energy and the reverse (e.g., roller coaster, pendulum).

**Big Idea 12: Motion of Objects**
- **SC.6.P.12.1**: Measure and graph distance vs. time for an object moving at constant speed, and interpret the graph (slope = speed).

**Big Idea 13: Forces and Changes in Motion**
- **SC.6.P.13.1** *(cited)*: Types of forces: contact forces, and forces at a distance (electric, magnetic, gravitational).
- **SC.6.P.13.2**: Law of Gravity. Every object pulls on every other object, and the pull depends on their masses and the distance between them.
- **SC.6.P.13.3**: An unbalanced force acting on an object changes its speed, its direction or both.

*Vocabulary:* kinetic energy, potential energy (gravitational, elastic), mechanical energy, conservation of energy, speed, velocity, distance, time, distance-time graph, slope, constant speed, force, newton (N), friction, normal force, tension, applied force, gravity, mass, weight, magnetism, electric force, balanced/unbalanced force, net force, acceleration, inertia.

## 5. Which grade 6-7 benchmarks are annually assessed on the Grade 8 Statewide Science Assessment (SSA)?

### Takeaway
The Grade 8 SSA, given in spring of grade 8, assesses NGSSS benchmarks from **grades 6, 7 and 8** in four reporting categories: **Nature of Science, Earth and Space Science, Physical Science and Life Science**. Historically the weights were about 19% / 27% / 27% / 27%. Many grade 6-7 benchmarks are tested either directly as "annually assessed" benchmarks or through "Also Assesses" groupings. Confirmed annually assessed grade 6-7 benchmarks include **SC.6.E.7.4, SC.6.E.7.5, SC.6.L.14.2, SC.7.L.17.2, SC.7.P.11.2, SC.7.E.6.2 (also assesses SC.6.E.6.1), SC.7.N.1.5 and SC.7.N.3.1**. The full official list was not retrievable this session.
