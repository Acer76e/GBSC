# Grade 6-7 Math & Science: Common Struggles, Prerequisite Gaps, Study Strategies, and Free Florida-Aligned Resources

Research note for a parent of a Florida 6th grader (and for building quiz topics in a study app). Researched 2026-10-30.

**Method caveat:** Fetching was blocked for several primary hosts in this session (ies.ed.gov, assessment.aaas.org, blog.khanacademy.org, stjohns.k12.fl.us, nsdl.library.cornell.edu), so several findings rest on search-result excerpts rather than full-text reads. Items drawn from the researcher's background knowledge and not verified this session are labelled **[unverified]** and kept in Inferences/Gaps, not Cited Findings.

## Q1. What are the most common grade 6-7 math misconceptions and difficulties, and which prerequisite gaps predict trouble in 7th grade math and pre-algebra?

### Takeaway
The best single predictor found is 5th-grade knowledge of **fractions and long division**, which predicted high-school algebra and overall math achievement even after controlling for family background. In grades 6-7, the most-documented conceptual stumbling blocks are: (1) reading the **equals sign** as "the answer comes next" instead of "is equivalent to"; (2) treating the **negative sign** only as subtraction; and (3) treating **variables as labels** (e.g., "s = students") instead of numbers.

### Cited Findings
**Prerequisite predictors**
- Fifth graders' understanding of fractions and division predicted high-school algebra knowledge and overall math achievement, even after statistically controlling for parents' education, income, and other background factors (Siegler et al., 2012, *Psychological Science*). — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/knowledge-of-fractions-and-long-division-predicts-long-term-math-success.html); [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2012/june/june15_mathsuccess.html)
- Data: a U.S. sample of 599 children tested at ages 10-12 (1997) and again at 15-17 (2002), and a U.K. sample of 3,677 children tested at 10 (1980) and 16 (1986). The pattern held in both countries despite data collected about 20 years apart. — [UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/early-grasp-fractions-long-division-predicts-high-school-math-achievement); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/06/120615114057.htm)
- Note: press coverage reports the **combined** fractions-and-division measure. A claim on a Mathnasium blog that fractions alone were a "stronger" predictor could not be verified. — [Mathnasium blog](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/cherryhills/news/fractions-algebra-connection)

**Fractions instruction (IES/WWC)**
- The WWC practice guide *Developing Effective Fractions Instruction for K-8* (NCEE 2010-4039) makes five recommendations. Rec. 1 is to build early fraction concepts on students' informal sense of sharing and proportionality, starting with equal-sharing activities. Rec. 5 covers teacher professional development. — [REL Midwest overview PDF](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/rel/fractions-grade-6/pdf/PracticeGuideOverview.pdf); [guide landing page](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/15)
- REL Midwest's Teaching Fractions Toolkit targets **6th-grade** teachers and uses "fractions" to cover all rational numbers, including decimals, percents, and negative fractions. That makes rational numbers as a whole the grade 6 priority. — [REL Midwest Fractions Grade 6](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/rel/fractions-grade-6)

**Algebra-readiness misconceptions**
- Middle schoolers' misconceptions about the equals sign and the negative sign both make it harder to learn to solve algebraic equations (Booth & Koedinger, 2008). Equals-sign misconceptions persist even among college calculus students (Clement, Narode & Rosnick, 1981). — [core.ac.uk review PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/77937877.pdf)
- Students with an incomplete understanding of the negative sign link it only to the binary operation of subtraction, and are more likely to use incorrect strategies on equations. — [core.ac.uk review PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/77937877.pdf)
- Longitudinal study of middle schoolers' understanding of the equal sign (Alibali, Knuth et al., 2007). — [Alibali lab PDF](https://alibali.psych.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/371/2018/02/AsquithAtephensKnuthAlibali2007.pdf)
- Variable misconceptions: students read "s" as a label for "students" rather than "the number of students" (Rosnick, 1981), or believe a variable cannot stand for more than one value (Knuth, Alibali, Weinberg & McNeil, 2005). — [Carleton "Adventures in Algebra" misconceptions page](https://www.math.carleton.edu/comps2016-17/Adventures-in-Algebra/sites.google.com/site/adventuresinalgebra/for-teachers/misconceptions-addressed.html)
- A study of seventh graders found some students do not understand the difference between the *value* and the *size* (absolute value) of two integers. — [METU abstract](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/07142a45-7267-4adf-870d-1310f825a5cc/prospective-teachers-knowledge-on-middle-school-students-possible-descriptions-of-integers)
- Bush & Karp (2013), *Journal of Mathematical Behavior*, is described as a comprehensive review of middle-school algebra misconceptions and is recommended as the master source for quiz distractors. — cited via [core.ac.uk review PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/77937877.pdf)

**Florida test context**
- FAST Mathematics grade 6 has 36-40 items. Grade 6 allows a **four-function calculator**; grades 7-8 use a **scientific calculator**. FAST is given three times a year (PM1, PM2, PM3). — [FLDOE Test Design Summary, FAST Math](https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/20102/urlt/TDS-FAST-Math.pdf)
- FLDOE publishes a Grade 6 B.E.S.T. Instructional Guide for Mathematics (B1G-M) for planning B.E.S.T.-aligned instruction. — [FLDOE B1G-M Grade 6](https://www.fldoe.org/file/7576/B1G-M-Grade-6.docx)

### Inferences
- **Quiz topics that should be top priority (rational numbers first):** fraction magnitude and the number line; equivalent fractions; adding and subtracting with unlike denominators; multiplying and dividing fractions (why "invert and multiply" works); decimal place value; dividing multi-digit decimals; converting among fractions, decimals, and percents; ratio and unit rate; integers on the number line, absolute value, and integer operations; the equals sign as balance; writing expressions from words; solving one-step equations.
- **[unverified, background knowledge]** Other classic errors useful as app distractors:
  - "Longer decimal = bigger" (0.125 > 0.5)
  - "Multiplication always makes bigger, division always makes smaller" (fails for numbers < 1)
  - Adding numerators and denominators (1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5)
  - Treating PEMDAS as strictly left-to-right letter order (multiplication always before division)
  - Ratio confusion: treating 2:3 as the fraction 2/3 of the whole, instead of 2/5 of the whole
  - Percent increase vs. percent of
  - −3 < −5 because "3 is smaller"
  - Confusing area with perimeter, and volume units (cm² vs. cm³)
  - Treating mean, median, and mode as interchangeable; ignoring outliers
- Because grade 6 FAST uses only a four-function calculator, **multiplication fact fluency and fraction computation** remain directly test-relevant. Fact fluency is a sensible warm-up module even though this session found no citation showing it predicts outcomes.

### Gaps
- Exact wording and evidence ratings of WWC fractions Recommendations 2-4 could not be fetched (ies.ed.gov blocked). From background knowledge **[unverified]**: Rec 2 is about recognizing fractions as numbers and using number lines; Rec 3 is conceptual understanding of computation procedures; Rec 4 is ratio, rate, and proportion problems.
- No primary-source statistics were found on how common each specific grade 6 misconception is (e.g., % of students who mis-order decimals).
- No direct citation was found for multiplication-fact fluency as a predictor of 7th-grade success.

## Q2. What are the most common grade 6-7 science misconceptions, and which science skills do students lack?

### Takeaway
The best-documented middle-school misconceptions are:
- **Force and motion:** a moving object needs a continuous force; force is confused with velocity.
- **Heat vs. temperature:** treated as the same thing; temperature thought to depend on mass.
- **Seasons:** believed to be caused by Earth's distance from the Sun.
- **Plant mass:** believed to come from soil rather than CO₂ from the air. This persists even after photosynthesis is taught.

Florida grade 6 science (M/J Comprehensive Science 1) begins with the Nature of Science, energy transfer, speed, and distance-time graphs. **Graph reading and practice with variables** are therefore immediate needs.

### Cited Findings
**Physical science**
- Force and motion, conservation of energy, and geometrical optics are areas with deep misconceptions that resist conventional instruction, and they feature prominently in U.S. middle-school standards. — [Singh & Schunn, Univ. of Pittsburgh LRDC](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/singhschunn-jpteo09.pdf)
- Students commonly think "temperature and heat are the same thing." — [science-education-research.com](https://science-education-research.com/category/conceptions/learning-impediments/grounded-learning-impediment/); [OpenLearn TI-AIE: Heat and temperature](https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/mod/oucontent/view.php?id=64798)
- Some learners believe an object's temperature depends on its mass. A study of Class VII (age ~12-13) students analyzed heat-expansion and temperature misconceptions. — [Semesta (UNP) grade 7 study](https://semesta.ppj.unp.ac.id/index.php/semesta/article/view/204)
- Harvard-Smithsonian MOSART provides validated misconception-based assessment items for middle-school physical and earth science. — [MOSART misconceptions](https://waps.cfa.harvard.edu/mosart/misconceptions.php)

**Earth/space science**
- A common misconception, even among adults, is that seasons are caused by Earth's distance from the Sun. — [Minnesota STEM Teacher Center, Sun as Energy Source](https://stemtc.scimathmn.org/node/1154)
- Florida grade 6 standards include SC.6.E.7.6 "Differentiate between weather and climate" and SC.6.E.7.7 "Investigate how natural disasters have affected human life in Florida." — [CPALMS M/J Comprehensive Science 1 (#2002040)](https://cpalms.org/PreviewCourse/Preview/56)
- Climate confusion (including weather vs. climate) is common enough that NCSE publishes teacher guidance on it. — [NCSE, Countering Climate Confusion](https://ncse.ngo/countering-climate-confusion)

**Life science**
- Most students do not understand that a plant's dry mass comes mostly from carbon dioxide, and this persists after studying photosynthesis. Only more advanced students consistently interpreted mass changes in plants or soil as evidence that matter moves. — [PMC study on plant growth/matter](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152202)
- Other plant misconceptions: plants need soil to survive, and plants use soil minerals as "food." — [same search cluster; NSTA blog, Overcoming Misconceptions](https://www.nsta.org/blog/overcoming-misconceptions-0)
- CPALMS has a grade 6 tutorial on "Levels of Organization" (cells → tissues → organs → systems), reflecting a grade 6 Florida life-science focus. — [CPALMS Levels of Organization](https://cpalms.org/PreviewResourceStudentTutorial/Preview/118882)

**Florida course scope and skills**
- M/J Comprehensive Science 1 (#2002040) covers plant systems, weather, plant and animal characteristics, the earth, electricity and magnetism, and forms of energy. — [Pasco course catalog](https://www.pasco.k12.fl.us/course_catalog/middle_school_course/m-j-comprehensive-science-1)
- One Florida district (St. Johns) lists these Quarter 1 topics for 6th grade standard science in 2026-27: Nature and Practice of Science; Energy Transfer and Transformation; Calculating Speed; Distance vs. Time Graphs. (Search excerpt only. Later quarters were not visible.) — [St. Johns 2026-27 YAG PDF](https://www.stjohns.k12.fl.us/year-at-a-glance/wp-content/uploads/sites/101/2026/07/6th-Grade-Standard-Science-2026-2027.pdf)
- The 2024 St. Johns pacing put forces in Q1 and Earth's spheres and atmosphere in Q2. — [St. Johns 2024 YAG](https://www.stjohns.k12.fl.us/year-at-a-glance/wp-content/uploads/sites/101/2024/07/6th-Grade-Standard-Science-YAG.pdf)
- CPALMS has a grade 6 "Models in Science" tutorial, which reflects the Nature of Science strand. — [CPALMS Models in Science](https://cpalms.org/PreviewResourceStudentTutorial/Preview/123013)

### Inferences
- **[unverified, background knowledge]** Additional misconceptions to quiz on:
  - Cells: all microbes are harmful; viruses are cells; plant cells don't have mitochondria.
  - Classification: whales are fish; bats are birds.
  - Plants "breathe in CO₂ and animals breathe in O₂," with respiration seen as animal-only. In fact plants respire too.
  - Heredity: traits are "blended"; acquired traits are inherited.
  - Energy is "used up" rather than transformed.
  - "Cold" flows into objects.
  - Heavier objects fall faster.
  - Plate tectonics: continents float on liquid magma.
  - Rock cycle: a fixed linear sequence.
- **Science skills to drill:** identifying independent, dependent, and controlled variables; reading and interpreting distance-time graphs and data tables; computing speed = distance/time (this links to math unit rates); writing Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) answers; and academic vocabulary.
- **Cross-subject link:** speed and unit rate, graph reading, and the mean of trial data are shared math and science skills. One app module could serve both.

### Gaps
- Project 2061's item-level misconception data (with % of students) could not be fetched (assessment.aaas.org blocked). From background knowledge **[unverified]**: AAAS's free Science Assessment site lists misconceptions by topic, with percent correct for middle-school students nationally. It would be the ideal distractor source.

- Whether Florida has adopted new science standards replacing NGSSS SC.6.* for 2026-27 was not confirmed. The CPALMS SC.6.E.7.x codes cited are NGSSS-era codes.
- No source was found quantifying student weakness in CER writing or variable identification specifically.

## Q3. Which evidence-based study techniques suit an app (retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, worked examples, feedback, mastery)?

### Takeaway
Dunlosky et al. (2013) rated **practice testing (retrieval)** and **distributed (spaced) practice** as the only two "high-utility" techniques. Highlighting, rereading, and summarization were rated low. The IES practice guide *Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning* (Pashler et al., 2007) adds:
- space learning over time
- alternate worked examples with problems
- use quizzes to promote learning
- help students judge what they don't yet know

Together these map directly onto a spaced-quiz app design.

### Cited Findings
- Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, evaluated 10 techniques. **Practice testing** and **distributed practice** received "high utility" ratings and benefit learners of different ages and abilities. — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html); [DOI 10.1177/1529100612453266](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/improving-students-learning-with-effective-learning-techniques)
- Rated **low utility**: summarization, highlighting, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text, and rereading. Highlighting and rereading are among students' most common strategies. — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2013-01-strategies-grade-popular-ineffective.pdf)
- IES Practice Guide NCER 2007-2004 (Pashler et al.) covers: spacing learning over time; interleaving worked-example solutions with problem-solving exercises; using quizzes to promote learning; helping students allocate study time efficiently; pre-questions; delayed judgments of learning; and using tests to find material that still needs study. — [WestEd DWW copy of the guide](https://dww-library-files.wested.org/files/5198508.pdf); [EBTN summary](https://ebtn.org.uk/?p=870)
- The guide rates each recommendation strong, moderate, or low based on the evidence, and is aimed at content-heavy subjects including science and mathematics. Quizzing and spacing are among its top methods for increasing learning and memory. — [Learning Forward research brief](https://learningforward.org/wp-content/uploads/2007/12/research-brief.pdf); [UNT archive of IES guides page](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090807172258/http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/publications/practiceguides)

### Inferences
- **App design implications:**
  1. Make every session a short retrieval quiz, not a reading session.
  2. Schedule review with expanding intervals (spaced repetition), e.g., 1 day → 3 days → 1 week → 2-3 weeks.
  3. Mix problem types within a session (interleaving), e.g., mix fraction ÷, decimal ×, and percent problems so students must pick the strategy.
  4. Show a worked example, then a near-identical problem to solve ("example-problem pairs").
  5. Give immediate corrective feedback that explains the *misconception* behind a wrong answer, using misconception-based distractors.
  6. Gate advancement on mastery, e.g., 80-90% over spaced attempts.
  7. Show a "check what you know" pre-quiz, and delayed confidence ratings to help the student judge what still needs study.
- **[unverified, background knowledge]** Dunlosky's three moderate-utility techniques are elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, and interleaved practice. A "Why is this true?" prompt after an answer applies the first two.

### Gaps
- Exact IES 2007 evidence levels per recommendation could not be fetched. **[unverified]**: spacing and quizzing were rated moderate to strong; worked examples moderate.
- No grade 6-7-specific effect-size data were collected this session. Most of the base research is with older students or lab settings. Dunlosky notes the high-utility techniques generalize across ages.
- The IES 2009 Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics (RtI) guide was not reviewed. **[unverified]**: it recommends explicit instruction, visual representations, and building fact fluency.

## Q5. What review sequence makes sense over a school year and in the summer before 7th grade?

### Takeaway
No published sequence for a Florida grade 6 at-home review was found. The evidence supports this order:
1. Fraction and division foundations first (strongest predictor of later algebra).
2. Then rational numbers and integers.
3. Then ratio and rates.
4. Then expressions and equations (equals sign, variables).
5. Then geometry and data.

All of it should run under spaced, interleaved retrieval. Science should follow the school's pacing (Florida districts start with Nature of Science, energy, and speed/graphs), with weekly cumulative review.

### Cited Findings
- Fraction and division knowledge in grade 5 predicts later algebra achievement, which justifies putting it first. — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/knowledge-of-fractions-and-long-division-predicts-long-term-math-success.html)
- Equals-sign and negative-sign misconceptions impede equation solving, which justifies addressing them before 7th-grade equations. — [core.ac.uk review](https://core.ac.uk/download/77937877.pdf)
- Spacing and quizzing are top-rated methods, which supports cumulative spaced review instead of one-unit blocking. — [Learning Forward brief](https://learningforward.org/wp-content/uploads/2007/12/research-brief.pdf); [APS 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)
- Florida grade 6 science Q1 (St. Johns 2026-27): Nature of Science, energy transfer, speed, and distance-time graphs. — [St. Johns YAG](https://www.stjohns.k12.fl.us/year-at-a-glance/wp-content/uploads/sites/101/2026/07/6th-Grade-Standard-Science-2026-2027.pdf)
- FAST PM1, PM2, and PM3 run across the year and serve as natural checkpoints. — [FLDOE TDS](https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/20102/urlt/TDS-FAST-Math.pdf)

### Inferences (a proposed plan, not from a single source)
**School year (math), running alongside class:**
- **Weeks 1-6 (diagnostic + foundations):** multiplication/division facts; fraction magnitude and the number line; equivalent fractions; fraction +/−/×/÷; long division; decimal operations.
- **Weeks 7-14:** fraction/decimal/percent conversions; ratios vs. fractions (part:part vs. part:whole); unit rate; percent of a number.
- **Weeks 15-22:** integers (number line, absolute value, comparing negatives, operations); coordinate plane; order of operations.
- **Weeks 23-30:** variables as numbers; writing and evaluating expressions; equals sign as balance; one-step equations and inequalities.
- **Weeks 31-36:** area of triangles and quadrilaterals; volume of rectangular prisms; surface area; mean/median/mode/range, outliers, and dot/box plots. Then a cumulative review before PM3.
- **Every week:** about 20% new material and 80% spaced, interleaved review of earlier topics.

**School year (science):** follow the district pacing guide. Each week, add one cross-cutting skill (variables, graph reading, CER) plus misconception checks for the current unit (forces, heat vs. temperature, seasons, photosynthesis/respiration, cells/microbes, weather vs. climate).

**Summer before 7th grade (about 8 weeks, 15-20 minutes a day, 4-5 days a week):**
- **Weeks 1-2:** fraction and decimal fluency (the Khan grade 5-6 units work well for this).
- **Week 3:** integers, all four operations (critical for 7th-grade rational-number operations).
- **Week 4:** ratios, rates, and percents (the lead-in to 7th-grade proportional relationships).
- **Week 5:** expressions, equations, and the equals sign.
- **Week 6:** geometry formulas and data/statistics.
- **Week 7:** science skills: variables, graphs, CER, and key vocabulary. Also preview 7th-grade life science if the district's grade 7 course is life-science heavy **[unverified]**.
- **Week 8:** a mixed cumulative practice test (FAST sample items).

### Gaps
- No authoritative published "summer before 7th grade" sequence was found. The plan above is synthesized from the cited evidence.
- Florida grade 7 science course content (M/J Comprehensive Science 2) and the grade 7 B.E.S.T. math benchmark list were not retrieved this session. They would be needed to tailor the summer preview precisely.
